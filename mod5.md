**Slides 1-2: Foundation**
- Spain ruled the Philippines 300+ years, unifying independent barangays under centralized Catholic Church influence
- Despite unification, resentment built against Spanish rule

**Slides 3-5: Government Structure**
- National: Governor-General (appointed by Spanish Crown) held executive, judicial, religious, and military power
- Local: Provinces (Alcadias) → Towns → Cities → Barrios, with Alcaldes Mayores as provincial governors
- Royal Audencia was the supreme legislative body

**Slides 6-7: Economic Institutions**
- **Encomienda System (1570)**: Spaniards received tribute rights from assigned territories; three types: Private, Royal, Ecclesiastical
- Not a land grant—it was the right to collect taxes from inhabitants

**Slide 8: Taxation**
- Tithe (10% income), Sanctorum (Church support), Tribute (tax to landlord, 8-15 reales)
- Paid in cash or goods (tobacco, chickens, gold, blankets, rice, etc.)

**Slide 9: Forced Labor (Polo y Servicio)**
- Men aged 16-60 obligated to 40 days unpaid labor; resulted in abandoned farms, family separation, illness, death

**Slide 10-11: Galleon Trade & External Threats**
- Main early revenue source (1565–early 1800s); connected Manila-Acapulco trade
- Challenges: Chinese pirates (Limahong, 1574), Portuguese/Dutch threats, Treaty of Zaragoza (imaginary boundary)

**Slide 12-13: British Invasion (1761-1762)**
- Spain's involvement in Seven Years War; British occupied Ermita and Bagumbayan
- Three competing Governor-Generals: Simon de Anda (Spanish resistance), Dawsonne Drake (British), Archbishop Rojo (acting)
- Treaty of Paris ended the war

**Slides 15-27: Economic Development & Trade**
- Philippine Development Plan emphasized sustainable growth
- **Tobacco Monopoly (1782)**: Established under Governor-General Basco; brought revenue to offset Spanish subsidy costs; operated 100 years
- **Real Compañía de Filipinas (1785)**: Royal Company for inter-colonial commerce; capital 8M pesos; revoked privileges in 1830, opening Manila to foreign trade

**Slides 28-38: Social & Cultural Transformation**
- **Catholicism**: Spanish conversion efforts; Santo Niño de Cebu first artifact (1521)
- **Pre-colonial society**: Datus/rajas, maharlika (nobility), timawa (freedmen), alipin (servile class)
- **Barangays**: Small kinship units (30-100 families)
- **Hispanization**: Surnames decreed 1849; Spanish language introduced 1565 but discouraged; ~10% population spoke it

**Slides 39-44: Writing & Script**
- Latin alphabet replaced baybayin; Doctrina Christiana (published in Tagalog & Spanish) taught Christianity and Latin alphabet
- **Abecedario**: Spanish-based alphabet with 28-32 letters; persists in Chavacano (Zamboanga/Cavite)

**Slides 45-65: Art & Literature**
- **Painting**: Religious icons → secular tipos del pais → portraiture; masters include Damian Domingo (miniaturismo), Juan Luna (Spoliarium—gold medal), Felix Resureccion Hidalgo (silver medal)
- **Sculpture**: Santos replaced anitos; retablos in churches; Juan de los Santos (17th-century pioneer)
- **Graphic Arts**: Engraving (1590s), woodcuts, estampas, estampitas distributed during fiestas
- **Literature**: Devotional works, Pasyon, corrido ballads, drama in regional languages; first formal academy 1821

**Slides 66-72: Education**
- **University of Santo Tomas (1605-1611)**: Oldest existing university in Asia; elevated to university 1645; made Pontifical University 1902
- Alumni include Jose Rizal, Philippine Presidents, Supreme Court justices
- Disrupted only during Philippine Revolution (1898-99) and Japanese occupation (1942-45)
