### **Part 1: Government & Social Structure** (Slides 1-18)

**Government Systems:**
- No centralized monarchy—power held by local **Datu** (Visayan) or **Maginoo** (Tagalog) chiefs
- Primary government: **Barangay** (30-100 families), derived from "balangay" (sailboat)
- Laws based on **traditions and customs** (unwritten) and decisions by Datu + council of elders (written)

**Social Classes:**
1. **Datu/Maginoo**: Wealthiest, executive, legislator, chief justice in peace; commander-in-chief in war; handles foreign affairs/treaties
   - Inherited or chosen by community (bravest, wealthiest, wisest)

2. **Timauas/Maharlika**: Intermediate class; landowners who served rulers, didn't work fields; freemen with own land, no tribute

3. **Slaves (Oripuen/Alipin)**: Three types with different obligations:
   - **Ayuey**: Work 3 days for master, 1 for self; worth 2 gold taes
   - **Tumarapoques**: Work 1 day per 4; pay 10 chicubites rice/year
   - **Tomataban**: Work 5 days/month or 5 chicubites rice/year; most respected

**Slavery causes**: Inherited status, war captives, debt, crime punishment, voluntary sale

### **Part 2: Marriage & Dowry System** (Slides 19-26)

**Dowry (Bigay)**: Gift from groom to bride's parents; can include carabao, land, jewelry, brass gongs

**Five Dowry Types:**
1. **Bigay-kaya**: Land, gold, dependents
2. **Panghimuyat**: Payment to parents for rearing the girl
3. **Bigay-suso**: Gift to wet-nurse who breastfed bride
4. **Himaraw**: Reimbursement for feeding costs during infancy
5. **Sambon**: Dowry to girl's relatives

**Courtship Process:**
- **Paninilbihan**: Initial courtship (showing patience/dedication)
- **Pamamalae/Pamamanhikan/Pamumulungan**: Parents negotiate final arrangements
- **Wedding**: Officiated by priestess/babaylan; uncooked rice thrown on couple

### **Part 3: Tattoo Traditions** (Slides 27-35)

**History:**
- Ancient practice by indigenous groups; nearly extinct after Spanish/American arrival
- Spanish called Visayans **"Pintados"** ("painted people") due to extensive tattoos (1521)

**Purpose:**
- Spiritual/magical protection and strength
- Reward for successful headhunt (warrior distinction)
- Mark social status; enhance beauty, fertility, and serve as clothing

**Methods:**
- Sharpened metal/thorn/bone tapped into skin, or skin pricked/cut with black powder rubbed in

**Notable**: Maria Whang-Od, "last Kalinga tattoo artist" in her 90s

### **Part 4: Clothing Traditions** (Slides 36-40)

**Men's Clothing:**
- Primary: **Bahaque** (cotton cloth around waist, 2 fathoms/6 feet)
- **Kanggan**: Sleeveless cotton jacket (morning wear)
- **Purones**: Fine multi-colored head scarves
- **Gold strips** as adornment

**Women's Clothing:**
- **Baro/Camisa**: Jacket with sleeves
- **Saya/Patadyong**: Long skirt
- **Tapi**: Red or white cloth over skirt
- **Crowns/garlands**: Made of tinsel from China
- Some wore plain taffeta and damask from China

### **Part 5: Betel Chewing Practice** (Slides 41-45)

**History & Use:**
- Widespread throughout Philippines (north to south)
- Cordillerans (Igorots) carry betel containers; constant chewing by older men
- Believed to reduce hunger and tiredness during long field work

**Composition:**
- **Areca nut** (betel palm fruit) + **betel pepper leaf** (Piper betel)
- Nut cut, dabbed with **lime** (burnt sea-shell), mixed with water/oil
- Wrapped in leaf to form quid
- Produces blood-red spittle

**Mix Ingredients**: Lime, pepper leaf, tobacco, cardamom, cloves, ginger
